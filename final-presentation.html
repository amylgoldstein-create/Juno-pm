Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

RocketShip PMs need this handled: CANDIDATE Juno works in the Jira triage queue to find duplicate tickets, draft new tickets from untracked customer signals, and recommend backlog priority based on product strategy and engineering capacity. A PM approves anything before it changes in Jira. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Insight synthesis: Juno finds duplicate tickets in the Jira triage queue and suggests clusters for a PM to approve.. Juno groups tickets that describe the same problem, shows me why, and lets me approve or reject a cluster in under 30 seconds without opening every ticket.

Pass bar

Yes on at least 9, No on at least 8, average of 4 or higher

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- Justification: any score of 1
- Any change made in Jira without a PM approval. A Slack DM or private customer detail shows up in a suggestion.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

When several customers report the same problem in different words, the PM knows it's one issue within a day, not after someone escalates it or manually observes it across multiple tickets over time.

Baseline

Time to spot a pattern is about 7 days today: the time from the first ticket about an issue to the day related tickets get linked together.

Target and review date

Within 1 business day. Review on Friday, December 11, 2026, about 6 weeks after launch.

Usage metric

At least 80% of Juno's suggestions get approved or rejected by a PM within 2 business days.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

Suggestions piling up or sitting untouched. That means PMs are skipping Juno's suggestions and Juno is becoming one more queue nobody checks.

Stop line

- time to spot a pattern is still more than 3 days, or
- fewer than half of suggestions get acted on, or
- the rubric's automatic fail happens more than once.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest part was juggling so many tools at once. During class I was moving between the course site, Zoom, Slack, GitHub, Lovable, and my own AI tool, and several of those were new to me. Learning the tools took a lot of the energy I wanted to spend on the concepts. Some topics also moved faster than I could keep up with, especially agentic loops and evals. I would have gotten more out of working through a few more examples together as a class before doing them on my own.

Key learnings

Treat an AI tool like a new employee. It needs very clear instructions on what to do, what not to do, where to look, and when to ask for help

Aha moment

I didn't have one big moment that changed how I see my role. My biggest takeaway was realizing how much I still have to learn about AI.
